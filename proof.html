Known distortions

Two reasons theconversion rate lies.

Both are visible in the captures above, and both are the reason this site leads with order counts instead.

August’s session count was inflated

August recorded 43,520 sessions against 16,349 in July. The dashboard’s sessions-by-location tile shows Singapore at 21.5K and China at 4.1K for that month; desktop sessions rose 268%. By September, Singapore had fallen to 789 and China to 818.

Conversion rate is orders divided by sessions, so roughly 25,000 sessions that were never going to buy dragged August down to 0.68%. Treat the 0.68% figure as a measurement artefact, not a business result.

Shopify changed how sessions are counted on 21 September 2026

The analytics page carries a notice: “Beginning Sept 21, 2026, sessions and related metrics may shift as Shopify Analytics enables better visibility into real shopper activity.” A Definition change marker appears on the September chart at that date.

Any conversion rate that spans 21 September is not strictly comparable with one from before it. Order, cart and checkout counts are unaffected.

Deliberately absent

No revenue, no customer data, no testimonials I did not earn.

The store’s sales figures exist and I have seen them. They are the client’s to publish, not mine, so no dollar amount appears anywhere on this site.

Nor does anything that could identify a customer. The captures on this page are aggregate analytics screens, which is why they are safe to show you at full size.

Nothing on this site is a projection of what your store would do. It is a record of what one store did, with the parts that are not flattering left in.
