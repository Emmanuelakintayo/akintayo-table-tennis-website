Design decisions

Five choices and the reasoning behind them

01A coaching business is not a shop

Paul David coaches table tennis. What he sells is time: in-home coaching, private lessons, group lessons and camp clinics — an hour or a day, at a stated price, with travel affecting some bookings.

None of that belongs in a cart. Paul is paid in person, so a checkout would have added a step that does not exist in his business and would have to be maintained forever. The site was built with no online payments, which made the design question a different one entirely: how do you get someone from ‘I want lessons’ to a message in his inbox, in as few steps as possible?

02Four services, priced in the open

Each service carries its name, its duration, its price and a plain description of who it is for, with travel fees noted where they apply. Coaches routinely hide pricing and lose the enquiry to the uncertainty — the visitor assumes it is expensive, or assumes asking commits them to something.

Publishing it does two jobs: it filters out the people who were never going to book, and it gives everyone else a reason to act now rather than ‘think about it’.

03One booking action per service

Every service has its own Book now button, and each one opens a pre-addressed email rather than dropping the visitor into a generic contact page. The service they picked is already the subject of the message.

No form to build, no form to maintain, no submissions disappearing into a plugin — and no ambiguity on Paul’s end about which service the enquiry is for.

04The equipment page, which is really a partner path

Rather than build a shop Paul would have to run, the site carries a Recommended Equipment page with three setups tiered by level: the GEWO CS Energy Control for youth starting out, the CS Energy Carbon Pro for adults and kids wanting more speed, and the Xolo Offensive combo with Neoflexx rubber for players serious about developing their game.

Each links through to GEWO USA — a properly stocked store I had already worked on — and carries a coach-specific code, PDCGEWO, for 12% off. The student gets a straight answer and a discount; the coach gets a credible recommendation and zero inventory.

05Built to be handed over

The unglamorous half of the project: services structured so Paul can edit them himself, payment configured to match how he actually gets paid, the domain connected, and ownership transferred to him at the end.

A site the client cannot change is a site that goes stale within a year and quietly stops representing the business.

What I am not claiming

No booking or revenue figures are published for this project, so none appear here. What it shows is that the same thinking applies away from ecommerce: understand how the business actually takes money, then build the shortest honest path to it — and notice where two clients can be worth more to each other than either is alone. The site is live; judge the execution there.
